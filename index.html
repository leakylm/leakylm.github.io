Key Findings

Finding 1 — Conversation Leakage

[Placeholder] The full text of user prompts, including sensitive health queries, was observed in outbound requests to Meta's Pixel endpoint — during normal and incognito browsing sessions.

Finding 2 — Incognito Bypass

[Placeholder] Platform-native "privacy modes" did not prevent tracker execution or payload transmission. Conversations initiated in incognito still triggered advertising network calls.

Finding 3 — Server-side Mirroring

[Placeholder] Several platforms use the Meta Conversions API, a server-to-server channel, meaning browser-level blocking tools cannot intercept these transmissions.

Finding 4 — Opt-out Ineffectiveness

[Placeholder] Disabling training data opt-outs, conversation history, and personalization settings had no measurable effect on third-party tracker activity.

Methodology

A standardized, reproducible protocol applied uniformly across all platforms and surfaces.

Instrument & Intercept

mitmproxy (mobile) and Puppeteer+stealth (web) intercept all outbound network traffic during controlled interaction sessions.

Standardized Test Prompt

A fixed health-related prompt is used identically on all surfaces, enabling detection of exact prompt text appearing in tracker payloads.

Condition Matrix

Each surface is tested across all combinations of auth state, platform privacy mode, browser private mode, and privacy toggle settings.

Tracker Detection

Outbound requests are matched against a curated tracker domain list. PII detection scans payloads for prompt text, emails, and identifiers.

HAR + JSON Archiving

Full session traffic is exported as HAR files and structured JSON for reproducibility and later third-party verification.

Cross-surface Comparison

Findings are unified into a normalized comparison table, enabling apples-to-apples comparison across all tested AI platforms.

FAQ

Common questions about the research and its implications.

Am I affected if I use these AI platforms?

[Placeholder answer] If you have used any of the tested platforms while entering sensitive queries, your conversation data may have been transmitted to third-party advertising networks. This includes sessions in "private" or "incognito" mode.

What data specifically was leaked?

[Placeholder answer] We observed varying degrees of leakage: from basic session identifiers and IP addresses to, in severe cases, the full text of conversation prompts appearing in advertising network payloads.

Does using a VPN or ad-blocker protect me?

[Placeholder answer] Browser-based ad blockers can intercept client-side Pixel requests, but cannot block server-to-server transmission via Meta's Conversions API. A VPN may obscure your IP but does not prevent the platform from relaying conversation content via server-side channels.

Have the affected companies been notified?

[Placeholder answer] We followed responsible disclosure. See the Disclosure Timeline for the current status of vendor notifications and responses.

How was this research conducted?

[Placeholder answer] See the full Methodology section. In brief: we used network interception tools (mitmproxy and Puppeteer) to capture all outbound traffic during standardized interaction sessions, then analyzed payloads for PII and conversation content.

What legal context exists around these findings?

[Placeholder answer] Doe v. Perplexity AI, Meta Platforms, Google (Case 3:26-cv-02803, filed March 31, 2026) independently confirmed Perplexity's embedding of Meta Pixel and other trackers. Our research provides technical corroboration and broader coverage across additional platforms.
